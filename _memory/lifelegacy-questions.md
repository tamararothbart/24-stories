# The Story Starter Kit — 50 Interview Questions
# Life Legacy Stories

---

## Category 1 of 10 — Roots & Where You Came From
*Your story begins long before you were born. These five questions reach back to the people, places, and journeys that shaped your family's earliest chapters — and the ones who made you possible.*

1. What is the very first memory you can recall — where were you, and what do you see?
2. Describe the home you grew up in: its sounds, its smells, the way the light fell in different rooms.
3. What do you know about how your grandparents' generation lived — and how they came to be where you were born?
4. Was there a relative who felt larger than life to you as a child? What made them so unforgettable?
5. What family story — something your parents told about their own lives — has stayed with you all these years?

---

## Category 2 of 10 — Childhood & The World Back Then
*The world looked entirely different when you were small. These five questions explore the texture of everyday life — the games, the rules, the freedoms, and the fears that quietly shaped who you became.*

6. What were you like as a child — shy or bold, mischievous or careful? What kind of thing got you into trouble?
7. What did summers look like when you were growing up? Paint the picture.
8. Who were your closest childhood friends, and what did you get up to together?
9. Is there something you desperately wanted as a child that you were told no to — and how did that play out?
10. What did you imagine your adult life would look like when you were ten years old?

---

## Category 3 of 10 — School Days & Finding Your Voice
*School is where we first discover who we are — and often, who we are not. These five questions revisit the teachers, the triumphs, the embarrassments, and the turning points of your formative years.*

11. Was there a teacher who genuinely saw something in you, or changed something in you? What did they do?
12. What were you known for at school — and was that reputation entirely fair?
13. Describe a moment from your school years that you've never quite forgotten.
14. Were you a rule-follower or a rule-bender? Tell me about something that proves it.
15. When did you first discover what you were good at — or what you were drawn to?

---

## Category 4 of 10 — Love, Courtship & Partnership
*The stories we tell about love are rarely the ones we planned. These five questions invite the real version — the nerves, the obstacles, the moment you said yes, and what you've understood since.*

16. Tell me about the moment you knew you were truly, properly in love.
17. How did you and your partner meet — and what was your honest first impression of them?
18. Were there any obstacles, rivals, or objections standing between you and the person you chose?
19. Describe a moment in your relationship that tested everything — and what it showed you about each other.
20. What do you understand about love now that you couldn't possibly have known at the start?

---

## Category 5 of 10 — Work, Ambition & Purpose
*A life's work is about far more than a job title. These five questions explore the ambitions you chased, the detours that surprised you, and the meaning you found — or carved out — along the way.*

21. What was the very first job you had — and what did it teach you about people, or about yourself?
22. Was there a moment in your working life when everything changed — a turning point you didn't see coming?
23. What did you build, create, or achieve that you are quietly most proud of?
24. Did you ever take a risk that paid off — or one that didn't? What happened after?
25. Looking back honestly, was the work you did the work you were meant to do?

---

## Category 6 of 10 — Becoming a Parent
*Parenthood transforms us in ways we never anticipated. These five questions capture the love, the terror, the fierce pride, and the particular wisdom that only comes from raising another human being.*

26. What was the moment you became a parent — what did it actually feel like?
27. Which of your children's childhood moments do you find yourself replaying most often?
28. What do you think you got right as a parent? And what would you approach differently?
29. Tell me about a conversation with one of your children that you've never forgotten.
30. What do you most want your children — and their children — to truly understand about who you are?

---

## Category 7 of 10 — Hard Times & How You Survived Them
*The chapters we'd rather skip are often the ones that define us most. These five questions make space for the losses, the failures, and the fierce resilience that quietly forged who you are.*

31. What is the hardest thing you have ever been through — and how did you keep going?
32. Was there a loss — a person, a dream, a way of life — that changed the shape of everything that came after?
33. Did you ever fail at something that mattered deeply to you? What happened next?
34. Who was there for you during your darkest period — and what did they do or say that helped?
35. What did your hardest years give you that you couldn't have found any other way?

---

## Category 8 of 10 — Adventure, Travel & The World Beyond Home
*The places we go — and the journeys we never planned — reveal something essential about who we are. These five questions open the atlas of a life, and a self, discovered in motion.*

36. Tell me about a place that stopped you in your tracks — somewhere that genuinely moved or changed you.
37. Was there a journey that went completely off-script? What actually happened?
38. Have you ever been somewhere so different from everything you knew that it shifted how you saw yourself?
39. Is there a place you carry with you still — a city, a landscape, a house — that you will never forget?
40. What is the most daring or spontaneous thing you ever did — and would you do it again?

---

## Category 9 of 10 — Joy, Celebration & The Good Times
*Not every story worth keeping is dramatic. These five questions reach for the moments of pure, uncomplicated joy — the ones that lit you up, the ones you'd step back into without a moment's hesitation.*

41. What has been the happiest period of your life — and what, exactly, made it feel that way?
42. Describe a celebration — a wedding, a birthday, a triumph — that you can still feel when you think of it.
43. What simple, ordinary pleasure have you loved your entire life?
44. Was there a moment of unexpected, unearned joy — something small that stopped you completely?
45. What has made you laugh harder than almost anything else?

---

## Category 10 of 10 — Wisdom, Values & What You Know Now
*What do we take with us, in the end? These five questions invite the distilled wisdom of a life — the convictions that held, the regrets made peace with, and the gifts meant for those who come after.*

46. What do you know now that you wish you could go back and tell your younger self?
47. Is there something you believe deeply — a value or conviction — that has never wavered?
48. What is the one thing you want the people who love you most to remember about you?
49. Is there something you have not yet said to someone you love — something that still needs to be said?
50. If you could leave a single piece of wisdom for your grandchildren, or for anyone who comes after you — what would it be?
