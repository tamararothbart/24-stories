# Editing Boundaries — 24 Stories TextEdit

These rules define the absolute limits of what the 24 Stories TextEdit editor may and may not do.

---

## The Governing Principle

Clarify, shape, and polish — but do not replace the speaker.

---

## ALLOWED vs FORBIDDEN — Worked Examples

### Example 1 — Removing filler and false starts

**Original (spoken transcript):**
> Um, I think the thing I remember most is, well, we were all sitting in the kitchen and my father had this way of, you know, not saying much, but when he did, everyone listened.

**Allowed edit:**
> We were all sitting in the kitchen. My father had a way of not saying much, but when he did, everyone listened.

**Why allowed:** Removes filler and strengthens readability without adding detail.

---

### Example 2 — Never add backstory or framing

**Original:**
> My father had this way of not saying much.

**Forbidden edit:**
> My father, a stern but loving man shaped by years of hardship, ruled the kitchen with silence.

**Why forbidden:** Invents personality, backstory, emotional framing, and tone.

---

### Example 3 — Keep paraphrase as paraphrase

**Original:**
> She told me I mustn't come back that night. I don't remember the exact words, but that was the message.

**Allowed edit:**
> She told me not to come back that night. I do not remember the exact words, but that was the message.

**Forbidden edit:**
> "Don't come back tonight," she said.

**Why forbidden:** The storyteller said they don't remember the exact words. Converting to quotation marks invents precision they did not claim.

---

### Example 4 — Never intensify emotion

**Original:**
> Everyone was laughing.

**Allowed edit:**
> Everyone was laughing. *(unchanged)*

**Forbidden edit:**
> The laughter crushed me.

**Why forbidden:** Intensifies emotion beyond what the storyteller supplied.

---

### Example 5 — Tighten while preserving emotional truth

**Original:**
> I was embarrassed. I mean, everyone was laughing, and I just wanted to disappear.

**Allowed edit:**
> Everyone was laughing. I wanted to disappear.

**Why allowed:** Removes "I mean" filler and tightens without adding or intensifying.

---

## Banned Phrases

Never use any of the following. If these appear in the original, they may be retained — but the editor must never introduce them:

- little did I know
- in that moment
- a wave of emotion
- forever changed
- cherished memories
- as I look back now
- it was then that I realised
- life is precious
- the tapestry of life
- the journey
- a testament to
- the rest is history
- the bonds of family
- deeply moved
- overcome with emotion
- my heart sank
- a lump in my throat
- bittersweet
- life-changing
- meant to be
- the circle of life
- I realised then that

---

## Never Invented

The editor must never add:

- Facts
- Scenes
- Backstory
- Dialogue not supplied
- What someone thought
- What someone felt
- What someone wore
- What someone believed
- What someone intended
- What someone noticed
- What someone remembered
- What someone realised
- What someone said
- Emotional insight not present or clearly implied
- Dramatic stakes not supplied
- Sensory detail not supplied
- Historical, social, or cultural context not supplied
- Forgiveness, healing, closure, or redemption not supplied
- The lesson
- The moral
- The wisdom

---

## Never Become

The editor must never make the storyteller sound like:

- A memoir blurb
- A motivational speaker
- An inspirational LinkedIn post
- A eulogy (unless the material is a tribute)
- A magazine profile
- A sentimental legacy-writing service
- A creative writing class
- A ghostwriter who makes everyone sound the same

---

## Sensitive Material Rules

Handle the following with restraint — do not heighten the language:

- Grief
- Trauma
- Family conflict
- Illness
- Death
- Regret
- Estrangement
- Addiction
- Shame
- Violence
- Family secrets

Do not:
- Make accusations stronger than the storyteller made them
- Diagnose anyone
- Insert therapeutic language
- Turn restraint into melodrama
- Turn pain into performance
